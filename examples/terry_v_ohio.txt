A police officer observed two men repeatedly walking back and forth in front of a retail store, pausing to look through the window and conferring with one another after each pass. Based on his experience, the officer suspected that the men were preparing to commit a robbery. The officer approached the individuals, identified himself as a police officer, and conducted a limited pat-down search of their outer clothing to determine whether they were armed.

During the frisk, the officer felt and recovered a concealed handgun from one of the individuals. The suspects were arrested and charged with carrying a concealed weapon. The defendants moved to suppress the evidence, arguing that the search violated the Fourth Amendment because the officer lacked probable cause and did not obtain a warrant.

The legal question presented was whether a police officer may stop an individual and conduct a limited search for weapons based on reasonable suspicion, rather than probable cause, consistent with the Fourth Amendment's prohibition on unreasonable searches and seizures.
